# Value Investing Framework

Use this reference for investment analysis, market cycle reviews, and thesis construction.

## 1. One-Line System

```text
Invest inside the circle of competence. Buy durable cash-flow assets when cycle and emotion cause wrong pricing. Use position sizing and discipline to wait for value realization. Protect capital when valuation and public enthusiasm become excessive.
```

The practical formula:

```text
long-term return = business quality x purchase price x cycle position x behavior discipline
```

## 2. Ten Axioms

1. The object is a business, not a ticker.
2. A good company is not automatically a good investment; wrong pricing creates excess return.
3. Stock return comes from earnings change and valuation change.
4. Valuation is ultimately payback period.
5. ROE is a central clue to quality, but must be decomposed.
6. Cash flow is closer to truth than accounting profit.
7. Macro is for judging water level, not predicting exact points.
8. A-share markets may reward broad-cycle timing, but frequent timing has a high skill threshold.
9. The main enemy is behavioral failure.
10. The first goal of a system is survival, then compounding.

## 3. Investment Decision Flow

Follow this flow:

```text
personal base -> market season -> industry position -> company quality -> valuation -> expectation gap -> execution -> review/exit
```

### Personal Base

Check whether the investor can hold:

- 3-6 months of living expenses outside risk assets.
- No near-term spending needs funded by equities.
- Basic family protection considered.
- No reliance on forced selling for daily life.
- Psychological tolerance for meaningful drawdowns.

### Market Season

| Season | Market State | Preferred Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Winter | low valuation, bad sentiment, weak earnings | research, DCA indices, buy quality gradually |
| Spring | policy/funding improves, assets stop falling | increase exposure, identify leaders |
| Summer | profit effect spreads, valuation rises | hold core positions, avoid overtrading |
| Autumn | high valuation, public excitement, leverage rises | take profits gradually, move defensive |

Different sectors can be in different seasons at the same time.

### Industry Position

Ask:

1. Is the industry in introduction, growth, maturity, decline, or cyclical reversal?
2. Is demand long-term or stimulus-driven?
3. Is supply constrained or expanding?
4. Are leaders taking more profit share?
5. Do policy, population, technology, rates, or FX change the profit pool?

Industry matters more for cyclicals, brokers, banks, insurance, and early technology. Company specifics matter more for branded consumer, internet platforms, mature pharma, and strong niche leaders.

### Company Quality

| Dimension | Core Question | Signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business model | can it keep making money? | margins, repeat purchase, asset intensity |
| Moat | why won't competitors take profits? | brand, cost, scale, network, license, switching cost |
| Growth | where does future profit come from? | market size, share gain, pricing, capacity, new products |
| Financial quality | is profit real? | operating cash flow, FCF, receivables, inventory, debt |
| Management | is capital allocation rational? | dividends, buybacks, capex, M&A, ownership |
| Cycle resilience | can it survive downturns? | low-cycle profit, cash reserves, cost control |

### Valuation

Start with payback period, then cross-check with asset-specific methods.

| Type | Main Valuation | Watch |
| --- | --- | --- |
| consumer leaders | PE, DCF, dividend growth | brand, pricing, repeat purchase |
| mature pharma | PE, DCF | pipeline, policy, cash flow |
| innovative pharma | PS, probability-adjusted pipeline | success probability, commercialization, cash burn |
| internet | PE, FCF, buybacks | user stickiness, ads, commerce, cloud |
| advanced manufacturing | PE, PEG, orders | technology, cost, overseas demand, cycle |
| banks | PB, dividend yield | NIM, NPL, provisions |
| insurance | PEV, PB, dividend | liability reform, investment return, rates |
| brokers | PB, turnover cycle | bull/bear, policy, risk appetite |
| cyclicals | PB, mid-cycle earnings | inventory, price, utilization, capex |
| indices | PE/PB percentile, ERP | market water level, liquidity, sentiment |

Payback intuition:

| Valuation | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| 5-8x PE | low growth may still work; check value trap risk |
| 10-15x PE | suitable for stable quality businesses |
| 15-25x PE | needs strong and visible growth |
| 25x+ PE | low error tolerance |
| 40x+ PE | usually unsuitable unless growth certainty is exceptional |

### Expectation Gap

Look for verifiable gaps:

- Market sees weak current earnings, but profit turning point is near.
- Market sees macro pessimism, but liquidity or credit improves.
- Market sees industry pain, but leaders gain share.
- Market sees cost pressure, but future operating leverage improves.
- Market sees cyclical downturn, but supply contraction is working.

Every expectation gap needs future evidence.

## 4. Macro Water-Level Dashboard

Use monthly unless the user asks for a live review.

| Indicator | Opportunity Signal | Risk Signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| PE/PB percentile | historical low | historical high |
| ERP | high risk premium | low compensation |
| M1-M2 | low and turning upward | high then falling |
| social financing | bottoming or improving | weakening |
| PPI | bottoming or improving | high rollover or deflation deepening |
| 10Y gov yield | falling/stable | rising quickly |
| FX | stable/appreciating | fast depreciation pressure |
| US yields | easing | sharp rise |
| turnover | mild volume from low base | high-volume euphoria |
| margin/leverage | subdued | rising quickly |
| fund issuance | cold | hot products everywhere |
| sentiment | pessimism/doubt | optimism/excitement |

## 5. Buy/Sell Matrix

| Quality | Price/Cycle | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| good | undervalued, pessimistic, cycle bottom | prioritize buying |
| good | fair, spring/summer | hold or buy gradually |
| good | expensive, public optimism | trim gradually |
| good | extreme valuation, thesis over-owned | wait |
| average | very cheap, clear cyclical reversal | small or basket position |
| average | fair | low priority |
| poor | looks cheap | avoid value trap |
| poor | hot and expensive | avoid |

## 6. Strategy Boundaries

### Index DCA

Use for broad indices or durable sector indices in low-valuation zones. Do not apply blindly to individual stocks. Use valuation-based take profit rather than stop loss.

### Individual Stock Left-Side Investing

Use only after deep research and with valuation margin of safety. If price falls, re-check the thesis before adding.

### Right-Side Trend Trading

Use for brokers, cyclicals, or strong trend themes. Start small, add on confirmation, and define stop rules. Do not convert a failed right-side trade into a "value investment."

### High Dividend

Prefer growing dividends backed by real cash flow. Static high yield plus declining business can be a trap.

### Bonds And Cash

Use as defense and rebalancing tools. Falling rates favor duration; rising rates favor short duration and cash. When equities are very cheap and dividend yields are attractive, equities may have better risk/reward.

## 7. Common Confusions

- Long-term holding does not mean never selling.
- Undervaluation does not imply immediate upside.
- Diversification means low-correlated risk sources, not simply many names.
- Technical analysis can help right-side execution but should not replace fundamentals.
- Macro is neither useless nor omnipotent; use it as a low-frequency context signal.

## 8. Final Three-Layer Model

| Layer | Focus | Failure Mode |
| --- | --- | --- |
| business | cash flow, ROE, moat | buying good companies too expensively |
| market | valuation, cycle, liquidity, sentiment | freezing at bottoms, chasing at tops |
| self | patience, discipline, sizing, risk | knowing but failing to act |

Mature value investing requires all three:

```text
business: I know what it is worth.
market: I know why it may be mispriced.
self: I know whether I can wait for repricing.
```
